Automation Foundry AI

An AI-powered knowledge and integration platform for robotics automation: turning fragmented engineering experience into reusable, searchable automation cases.

Luis Alberto Muñoz Ubando

Founder & CEO (sole founder to date) · Tecnológico de Monterrey

Project portal ↗ Code ↗ Existing applications (IdeaFactory) ↗

TL;DR. Industrial engineering teams repeatedly spend weeks researching technologies and solving problems that others have already solved. Automation Foundry converts technical videos, engineering reports, vendor documentation and project experience into structured cases that engineers can search in natural language, compare, and reuse. The technical components already run as separate applications; the integrated robotics platform is still a prototype and has not been commercially validated.

1. The problem: engineering knowledge is fragmented

Robotics is advancing quickly, but deploying it in production remains complex, expensive and dependent on individual expertise.

Valuable automation knowledge is scattered across engineering teams and individual experts, technical reports and project documentation, demonstration and training videos, robotics manufacturers and technology vendors, research laboratories and academic publications, and previous implementations, including unsuccessful experiments.

Much of it is difficult to search, compare, validate or reuse. As a result, engineers repeat work that has already been done elsewhere, which raises development costs and delays industrial deployment. The bottleneck is less the availability of technology than the difficulty of turning existing technology and engineering experience into reusable solutions.

2. Our approach: reusable engineering intelligence

Each automation case captures the original problem and its operational constraints, the alternative strategies and technologies considered, the hardware, software, AI models and integration architecture, implementation challenges and corrective actions, measured performance and cost, and lessons learnt.

  1. Engineers describe an automation challenge in natural language.
  2. The platform retrieves comparable implementations and the technologies they used.
  3. Engineers see what worked, what failed and under which conditions.
  4. Verified experience is contributed back, making the knowledge base more useful for the next team.

Companies can also run private knowledge bases, keep institutional expertise in-house, and selectively share anonymised lessons with a wider professional network.

3. Method and technical stack

The platform is built on components that already run in production as separate applications. The integrated workflow connects them into one robotics-specific product.

Current foundation

Python, PyTorch and CUDA on NVIDIA DGX Spark hardware, with local language models served through vLLM, retrieval-augmented generation, multimodal AI processing, and API-based application services.

Current applications already support video transcription, technical knowledge extraction, semantic retrieval, conversational interfaces and automated document generation.

Planned platform

A Python/FastAPI backend, structured automation-case schemas, relational databases, vector search and evidence-grounded AI capabilities.

Integration with ROS 2 and NVIDIA Isaac Sim, supporting both cloud-based and privacy-preserving on-premises deployments.

Video knowledge extraction Semantic search Retrieval-augmented generation Automation case schema ROS 2 Isaac Sim On-premises deployment

4. Research direction: human–AI interaction

The most ambitious aspect of the project is moving beyond conventional AI assistants toward interactive, bidirectional collaboration between engineers and robotic systems.

Rather than only answering questions, engineers should be able to observe robotic processes as they unfold, understand the decisions being made, intervene when necessary, and supervise execution in real time. The AI system should also ask for guidance when it meets uncertainty, unexpected conditions, or decisions that require engineering judgement.

An early prototype with one of my PhD students: an intelligent Pac-Man character that, when threatened, asks its human supervisor which way to go.

The example is deliberately simple, but it captures the principle: an autonomous agent should not execute instructions blindly, and it should recognise when human intervention could improve its decisions. The same concept could eventually apply to industrial robots facing unexpected obstacles, agricultural machines in unfamiliar conditions, or surgical systems that need expert supervision.

Status: This interactive supervision layer is an exploratory research objective, not a validated platform capability. The open questions are which existing technologies can be integrated, which components must still be built, and how to turn early demonstrations into reliable, safe and scalable engineering workflows.

What this does not show yet: Automation Foundry as an integrated product has not been commercially validated. There are no verified paying customers or established revenue for the platform. The usage above demonstrates the technical building blocks, not product-market fit.

Why we believe this problem is worth solving

The idea came from users. Users of the IdeaFactory tools repeatedly asked for an integrated workflow that connects knowledge extraction, technical research and documentation. The developer has also observed the problem directly: at a large manufacturer in Mexico, engineers struggled to find which solutions had already been tested in the company or its supplier network, and supplier knowledge gaps were discovered only during projects.

This is still a hypothesis. Structured interviews with robotics integrators and paid pilots are the next step.

6. Market and business model (hypotheses)

The planned product is a B2B software subscription for robotics integrators, industrial engineering teams and manufacturers. The initial offering covers private project knowledge bases, AI-assisted extraction of automation case studies, semantic search, evidence-backed recommendations and controlled collaboration.

Annual subscriptions are expected to be tested at roughly $10,000–$30,000 for smaller engineering teams, with enterprise and on-premises deployments priced separately. These are pricing hypotheses, not validated customer commitments, and pricing will be tested through customer discovery and paid pilots before a model is committed.

Positioning

Alternatives include enterprise knowledge-management platforms such as Microsoft SharePoint and Copilot, Glean and Confluence, engineering forums, robotics vendors' technical resources, and general-purpose AI search tools. These help people store, search or discuss information, but they are not designed to turn automation experience into comparable, evidence-backed engineering cases.

The differentiation is a specialised workflow and, over time, a growing collection of structured, verified automation experiences. The central hypothesis is that reusable engineering experience, rather than generic AI-generated advice, is what automation teams will pay for.

Initial focus

Machine tending Kitting Line replenishment Mixed-object handling Later: agriculture, logistics, healthcare

The approach is hardware-agnostic and designed to complement existing robotics ecosystems, including ROS 2, NVIDIA Isaac Sim, Cosmos and emerging Physical AI technologies. Success will be measured through reduced engineering time, faster integration, improved reliability and demonstrable operational value.

7. Roadmap and next steps

  1. Integrated prototype. Connect extraction, case schemas and search into one robotics-specific workflow that turns engineering videos, technical documentation and project experience into searchable automation cases.
  2. Customer discovery. Run structured interviews with robotics integrators and industrial engineering teams to learn which workflows are most valuable, whether customers will pay, and what would make teams willing to share experience.
  3. Paid pilots. Validate the workflow and pricing with a small number of integrators or engineering teams.
  4. Initial case library. Build a set of high-quality, verified automation case studies as the basis for the network.
  5. Platform expansion. Add private knowledge bases, permission-controlled collaboration and ROS 2 / Isaac Sim integration, then research human-supervised robot autonomy.
