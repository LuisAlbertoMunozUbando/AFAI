Business Model Canvas.

Physical AI Engineering Copilot — helping robotics integrators and industrial engineering teams reuse proven engineering knowledge, reduce repetitive work, and move from technical uncertainty to faster, better-informed automation deployments.

Stage disclosure: This canvas is a proposed business model, not evidence of commercial traction. The IdeaFactory applications provide a technical foundation; AFAI's integrated robotics product, pricing and customer demand still require pilot validation.

1 · Key Partners

  • Industrial robotics integrators and pilot customers
  • Robot, sensor and automation vendors
  • Manufacturers and engineering associations
  • Universities, laboratories and domain experts
  • Cloud, edge-compute and simulation ecosystems

2 · Key Activities

  • Extract and structure engineering cases
  • Build retrieval and recommendation workflows
  • Validate outcomes with pilot integrators
  • Maintain evidence, privacy and provenance
  • Develop reusable integration playbooks

3 · Key Resources

  • Robotics and AI domain expertise
  • Existing IdeaFactory applications and workflows
  • Engineering case library and evaluation data
  • GPU/edge infrastructure and AI stack
  • Customer relationships and trusted field feedback

4 · Value Propositions

  • Reduce duplicated engineering effort by finding relevant prior implementations
  • Accelerate feasibility and integration with evidence-backed technical options
  • Preserve organisational know-how from videos, reports, failures and lessons learnt
  • Reduce decision uncertainty with comparable cases and traceable sources
  • Protect proprietary knowledge through private workspaces and selective sharing (planned)
  • Enable future Physical AI workflows by connecting knowledge with reusable capabilities

5 · Customer Relationships

  • Founder-led discovery and pilot onboarding
  • High-touch support for early integrators
  • Team subscriptions and knowledge stewardship
  • Feedback loops based on engineering outcomes
  • Enterprise success and security reviews

6 · Channels

  • Direct B2B outreach and industry introductions
  • Demonstrations using real engineering cases
  • Pilot programmes with integrators
  • Technical workshops and professional networks
  • Partner referrals and application website

7 · Customer Segments

  • Beachhead: industrial robotics system integrators
  • Manufacturing automation engineering teams
  • Later: OEM application engineers and robotics solution providers
  • Later: logistics, agriculture and specialised sectors
  • Economic buyer: engineering director / integration lead; daily user: field or applications engineer

8 · Cost Structure

  • AI engineering and product development
  • Compute, inference, storage and data processing
  • Security, compliance and customer-specific deployment
  • Pilot delivery, technical support and onboarding
  • Sales, partnerships and customer acquisition

9 · Revenue Streams

  • Annual B2B team subscriptions (pricing hypothesis: US$10k–30k/year)
  • Enterprise and on-premises licensing, priced separately
  • Paid pilot / onboarding / integration services where appropriate
  • Later: premium domain-specific workflows and partner offerings
  • No revenue assumptions presented as validated sales
Customer economics

How AFAI creates measurable customer value

01 · Capture

Turn scattered knowledge into assets

Convert project reports, training videos, vendor material and engineering notes into searchable cases with provenance.

02 · Discover

Find relevant precedents

Surface comparable projects, hardware choices, constraints, implementation issues and lessons learnt.

03 · Decide

Shorten technical evaluation

Help engineers compare options and formulate testable integration plans; qualified engineers remain accountable.

04 · Reuse

Compound knowledge over time

Feed validated outcomes back into the organisation's knowledge base so the next project starts further ahead.

Illustrative value model

What a customer can measure

Engineering hours saved

Time spent researching and comparing solutions before vs. after using AFAI.

Time to first viable design

Elapsed time from problem definition to a technically reviewable integration approach.

Knowledge reuse rate

Share of new projects that cite or adapt an existing validated engineering case.

Illustrative ROI calculation — not a forecast

If a team saves 10 engineer-hours per week at an internal fully loaded cost of US$75/hour, annual gross time value is approximately US$39,000 (10 × 75 × 52). At a hypothetical US$20,000 annual subscription, the simple gross value-to-fee ratio is 1.95× before implementation, verification, training and other costs. All inputs must be measured with pilot customers; saved hours do not automatically translate into cash savings.

Go-to-market

Start narrow. Prove value. Expand.

Initial wedge: industrial robotics integrators

Start with one repeatable workflow: an engineer describes an automation problem, retrieves comparable documented cases, compares technologies and produces a source-backed technical shortlist.

Why this segment? Integrators repeatedly face new applications, fragmented supplier knowledge and time-sensitive feasibility decisions.

Proposed commercial motion

Discovery interviews → bounded pilot with approved documentation → baseline and after-use measurement → paid team subscription if value is demonstrated → expansion to private enterprise knowledge bases.

Commercial status: pilot conversions, willingness to pay, retention and pricing have not yet been validated.
